FAQ

The common questions about farming, groups, regions and achievements.

How does farming work?

Farming is playing a public match in a controlled way so everyone can repeatedly get the actions they need for Bloodpoints, grades, achievements or Tome/quest progress instead of playing the match normally.

What is the difference between organized and Asian farming?

Organized farming is planned: everybody is there for the farm, normally in VC, and the group uses known methods. Asian farming is easier to enter alone but is random, slower and less reliable.

How do I find a group?

Use the Bloodpoint Farming Discord. It is the biggest and most active farming community I know for this.

How can I change my region?

The easiest tool for this is Make Your Choice (MYC). It lets you select the matchmaking region you want to use.

What offerings do I need?

Bloody Party Streamers or anniversary cakes are the normal expectation. In Asian farming they are basically required in practice. In organized farming other BP offerings can be accepted if everybody agrees first, but they are generally still unwanted.

What perks are common?

Reactive Healing and Self-Care are used a lot. Asian farming also commonly uses Any Means Necessary. For healing-heavy organized methods, everybody should have Reactive Healing, while only one Survivor needs Leader.

How long does a farming round take?

Asian farming is usually around 10 minutes when it goes correctly. Organized methods can be much faster or slower depending on whether the group is optimizing BP/hour, BP/offering, grades or a particular achievement.

Can I do achievements in custom games?

No. The achievement methods here are for public matches, which is why the organized group queues into each other rather than doing everything in a custom lobby.

Can I do achievements while farming?

Yes. Quite a few are much easier when everybody knows the setup. Tell the group what you need before the match; some achievements only need a tiny change to the normal farm while others are better as a dedicated round.
